# F-18

The F-18 demo was written in 1996 with IRIS Performer 2.2 and Coryphaeus
EasyScene (IRIX executable `F18.pf22_ogl62`). Its models, its terrains and
its cockpit were made with Coryphaeus' Designer's Workbench and are stored
in its binary format, `.dwb`, loaded by `pfdLoadFile_dwb`. This directory
holds a loader for that format, rebuilt from the loader linked in the
executable, and a viewer (`f18_view`) using the Performer layer written for
Mekton (`src/mekton/pf.c`).

## The demo

    f18 [-D symbol] [script.set]

`f18` runs an EasyScene script of the demo, by default
`data/f18/scripts/F18_1pipe.set` (the San Diego coast); `LB_1pipe.set` is
Long Beach, and the `3pipe` scripts make a panorama of three screens side
by side. `-D` defines a symbol for the `%ifdef` of the scripts (`MORRIS`
enables the network part of the F-18 scripts).

### EasyScene scripts

EasyScene (Coryphaeus) runs an application from a script: its parser was
generated by yacc and lex in the executable, so `escene.c` is rebuilt from
the scripts of the demo and from the messages of the executable. A script
is a list of statements ended by `;`, with `#` comments and `%ifdef`,
`%ifndef`, `%else`, `%endif`, `%define` directives:

| Statement | Effect |
|---|---|
| `path "dir:dir..."` | the search path of the models, textures and sounds; the directories are looked for from the current directory and from the demo directory (`./data/models` is `data/f18/models`) |
| `process`, `cpu`, `phase`, `framerate` | the processes of Performer: one process here |
| `pipe "name" add on screen N, viewport x0, x1, y0, y1` | a screen; the window has its size (reduced to fit the screen), the pipes are side by side |
| `qdev "DEVICE" function "func" [, autorepeat]` | a key (IRIS GL device names: `WKEY`, `UPARROWKEY`, `LEFTALTKEY`...) calls a function |
| `module "name" add, preinit "func", postapp "func"...` | functions called at the phases of the frame: `preinit` and `init` at `configure`, `postinit` at the end of the script, then `preapp`, `postapp`, `precull`, `postcull`, `predraw`, `postdraw`, `postrender` each frame |
| `configure` | opens the window |
| `object "name" load "file" [isectGeom "file"] [mask N]` | loads a model under a DCS (a model loaded twice is cloned) |
| `object "name" loadstatic "file"`, `object "name" type terrain` | a model without DCS, the ground |
| `channel "name" add on pipe "pipe"` | a view on a pipe |
| `channel "name" viewport l r b t, clip near, far, fov h v, fadeLOD, viewpos x y z, viewrot h p r` | its area, clipping planes, field of view (-1: from the aspect), offset from its object |
| `channel "name" attach "object"` | the view follows an object (the camera) |
| `userCallback "func" argument "string"`, `usercb` | calls a function with a string |

The functions named by a script are found by name, as the original found
the symbols of the executable: the program registers them with
`esRegisterFunction`. The functions of the demo that are not rebuilt yet
(the missiles, the gun, the radar display, the
weather `esInitEnv`, the smoke, the trails, the sound...) are reported
once by the interpreter, which goes on. Without `initFlight`, the eye
would be moved with the mouse (left drag: turn, right drag or wheel:
speed, middle drag: slide).

### The flight

`flight.c` is rebuilt from the functions of the executable named by the
scripts and from the ones they call (`initFlight`, `updateFlight`,
`planeCrash`, `dispFlight`, `eyeFlight`, `watchEnemy`, `toggleWingman`,
`noSpeeding`, `restartFlight`, `toggleHud`, `plusRadar`, `readRoute`,
`initEnemy`, `updateEnemy`) and from the "wingman" views of EasyScene
(`esWingmanFollow`, `esWingmanTether`, `esWingmanTether2`).

The demo moves its objects by a step per frame at the frame rate of the
script (60 Hz): `f18` calls the app modules at that rate.

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| mouse position | from the middle of the window: roll (5°/frame at the edge) and pitch (1°/frame); the pitch turns the airplane around its wings |
| arrows | the same from the keyboard: left / right roll (3°/frame), up dives, down climbs (0.7°/frame) |
| `M` | the mouse flies the airplane or not (for a touchpad: fly with the arrows) |
| `A` / `Z`, right / middle button, wheel | faster / slower (1 m/s per frame, 10 m/s a wheel step, 100 to 1000 m/s) |
| `E` | the views around the airplane in turn: tether (fixed side), tether 2 (turning with the airplane), follow (the place of the airplane 30 frames ago, the view at start) |
| `P` | the view of each enemy in turn, then the cockpit |
| `F` | the airplane 20 lengths behind an enemy chosen at random |
| `N` | the enemies at their lowest speed |
| `R` | the head up display (HUD) on and off |
| Page Up / Page Down, `+` / `-` | range of the radar (the up and down arrows of the scripts) |
| left / right Alt, `1` / `2` | the left / right missile (not rebuilt yet) |
| space | after a crash, starts again at once (in flight: the gun, not rebuilt yet) |
| `W`, `T`, `S`, `H` | wireframe, textures, statistics, list of the keys |
| Esc | quit |

The keys of the scripts come first, except `A`, `Z` and the arrows, which
fly the airplane in `f18`: in the 3-screen script, where `Z` toggled the
textures, the textures have no key.

The ground is tested under the airplane every 20 frames, every frame below
500 m: below 5 m the airplane crashes ("You just died...Attempting to
restart mission"), goes up 500 m and starts again 5 seconds later. The
display shows the sight (a circle when an enemy is within 2 degrees of the
nose: "Locked on Enemy N"), the messages, the speed, the heading and the
height above the ground ("hat").

The five enemies start at fixed places (Enemy0 at 2000 m over the origin,
the others at the corners of the terrain) and replay recorded flights:
`N.route` files of 12-byte records (per frame: the roll rate, the pitch
rate and the speed, big-endian floats, the format in which the demo
records the flight of the player in `rec.pos`). They leave their route to
stay between 1000 and 3000 m above the ground (wings level, climb or dive
to 1250 m, nose level, roll back) and inside the terrain (roll 90 degrees
and turn to the origin). A crashed enemy turns into its wreck
(`Broken%d.dwb`) and comes back 30 seconds later. The route files are
`data/f18/0.route` to `4.route` (the demo opened them in its directory,
`./` in the path of the scripts); `0.route` and `2.route` are the same
flight. When a route file is missing, `f18` makes a route up from turns,
climbs and straight lines.

### The head up display

The Long Beach script loads the HUD of the demo, `myf18.drt` (the San
Diego scripts have it commented out). EasyScene draws it on a HUD channel
that follows the main channel, 2.5 m in front of the eye (`esSetHud`),
without depth, over every view; `updateHudValues` fills its variables
(`hudVars`, in the order of `easyScene.vars`): the position in EasyScene
coordinates, the pitch and the roll, the heading in tenths of degrees, the
speed, the height above the ground, the enemy locked, the loading and the
color of the two missiles (10 and green when loaded) and the "in range"
light. The HUD shows the pitch ladder turning with the roll, the heading
tape, the speed, the altitude and the missiles.

`.drt` files ("runtime" files of Designer's Workbench) are `.dwb` files
with links: rotations, translations, scales, visibility, colors and texts
of their groups, faces and strings, driven by functions of the variables.
`drt.c` was rebuilt from `f18hud.drt` and its ASCII version
`f18hud.drta`, which name the records:

| Opcode | Record | Fields used |
|---|---|---|
| `0x1775` | face | `+0x0a` 1 filled, 2 closed outline, 3 open line |
| `0x17c0` | string | `+0x04` color index (high 16 bits), `+0x10` font width, `+0x14` font height, `+0x20` alignment (2 centered), `+0x58` position |
| `0x179a`, `0x1799` | its text, its font (`olddwb.vfn`, polylines in a cell) |
| `0x2713` | link table | `+0x00` number of links of the record before it |
| `0x2787` | rotate (in the XY plane) | `+0x00` origin, `+0x14` lower and upper limits of the value, `+0x1c` start and end angles, `+0x24` flags (0x10000: limits) |
| `0x278f` | translate | `+0x0c` direction, `+0x18` start and end offsets, `+0x20` lower and upper limits, `+0x28` flags |
| `0x2790` | visibility | shown while the value is not negative |
| `0x278a` | text | `+0x0c` 0x10000 float, else integer; low bit: spaces, else zeros; its format in `0x2714` |
| `0x2788` | scale | `+0x00` origin, `+0x0c` axis |
| `0x2776` | color | the value is the color index |
| `0x2718` | function (80 bytes, outer first) | `+0x00` offset of the variable, `+0x08` function (high 16 bits), `+0x14` a, `+0x18` b, `+0x20` 2: of the next record, 3: of the variable |

The functions are 16 `x`, 7 `linear` a·x + b, 4 `div` x / a + b, 11
`mod2` a·x modulo b and 15 `trunc` trunc(x / a) + b (the five labels of the
heading tape check them: `linear(10, trunc(10, mod2(1, linear(1, div(10,
heading, 0), 370), 360), 0), 0)` is the multiple of 10 above the heading).
A color index c is the entry c / 128 of the color table (`0x1790`) at the
intensity c % 128 / 127: 250 is the red of a missile being loaded, 600 the
green of a loaded one.

## The viewer

    f18_view [data directory | model.dwb ...]

Without arguments the models are looked for in `data/f18/models` (the
`models` directory of the original distribution); a directory or `.dwb`
files can be given instead.

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| left drag, arrows | turn around the model |
| right drag, wheel, `+` `-` | zoom |
| middle drag, shift + left drag | pan |
| `n` `p`, page down / up | next / previous model |
| `w` | wireframe |
| `t` | textures |
| `l` | lighting |
| `d` | levels of detail: on the eye distance, or always the most detailed |
| `r` | reset the view |
| `h` | information and help |
| `q`, Esc | quit |

The textures are looked for, by the base name of the file name stored in
the model, next to the model, in `../textures` and in `textures`, with
the extensions `.rgb`, `.rgba`, `.int`, `.inta` and `.bw` when the name
has none.

### Encrypted textures

Most images of the demo were encrypted for its distribution, and the demo
loads its textures with `mypfLoadTexFile`, which decrypts them
(`ScrambleImageToImage`, `InitScrambleSeeds`, `ScrambleImageRow` in the
executable). The loader decrypts them the same way:

- an encrypted image is a verbatim SGI image (storage 0) whose header has
  4 in the word at 0x6c, after the colormap word; the run-length encoded
  images (such as `hl_road.rgb.unenc`) and the other values (9 or 10 for
  the smoke and exhaust images) are plain;
- the pixels are XORed with a table of 1633 seeds: seed 0 is 0x67 and seed
  i is 103 × seed i−1 modulo 256;
- the rows are taken from the bottom up and the pixel i of a row uses the
  next seed of the table (back to the first after the last) for the pixel
  i of the channels 0 and 2 and for the pixel width−1−i of the channels 1
  and 3.

XOR is its own inverse: the same function encrypts and decrypts.

## The Designer's Workbench format

A file is a sequence of big-endian records: a 16-bit opcode, a 16-bit
length of the data, then the data. `0x17ca` (push) and `0x17cb` (pop)
enclose the children of the record before them.

| Opcode | Record | Fields used |
|---|---|---|
| `0x1771` | header (320 bytes) | `+0x00` short units (0 m, 1 ft, 2 in, 3 cm, 4 nautical miles...), `+0x04` float version, `+0x08` date, `+0x86` short up axis: 2 for the models made with Y up (the airplanes, nose along -Z), turned to the Z up of Performer by a pitch of 90 degrees, as `pfdLoad_dwb` does |
| `0x1772` | group (160 bytes) | `+0x34` flags: bit 26, the children are decals on the first child (`pfLayer`) |
| `0x1773` | level of detail (96 bytes) | `+0x08` switch out, `+0x0c` switch in, `+0x10` center |
| `0x1775` | polygon (76 bytes) | `+0x00` color index, `+0x04` vertices, `+0x10` texture (-1 none), `+0x2c` color `0xAABBGGRR`, `+0x36` material (-1 none) |
| `0x177a`, `0x177c` | vertices of the polygon | 44 bytes each: color index, x y z, normal, s t, 2 floats |
| `0x1798` | name of the record before it | |
| `0x179c` | file name of the texture before it | |
| `0x17ca`, `0x17cb` | push, pop | |
| `0x17ea` | material (160 bytes) | `+0x04` diffuse, `+0x10` ambient, `+0x1c` shininess, `+0x20` specular, `+0x2c` emission, `+0x38` alpha |
| `0x17eb` | texture | `+0x00` width, `+0x02` height, `+0x04` components |

The other records (`0x1790` color table, `0x1791` eyepoints, `0x17e8`,
`0x17ec` and `0x17ed` settings, `0x17ee` texture mappings, `0x1799`,
`0x179a` and `0x17c0` texts of the cockpit) do not change the geometry and
are skipped.

The loader builds a `pfGroup` per group, a `pfLayer` per decal group, a
`pfLOD` (with a group child) per level of detail, and gathers the polygons
of a group sharing a texture, a material and a transparency into one
geoset of `PFGS_POLYS`. The polygon colors are kept with the materials
(color material on ambient and diffuse), as Performer does.

For this loader the Performer layer (`src/mekton/pf.c`) gained LOD ranges
and centers (`pfNewLOD`, `pfLODRange`, `pfLODCenter`, `pfLODScale`) and
decal layers (`pfNewLayer`, `pfLayerMode`, drawn with a polygon offset);
the nodes loaded from the Mekton and Certain Impact files keep their
behavior.
